## Proposed Changes

### Change 1: CITE for financial aid complexity (Introduction)

**Location:** "The complexity of the financial aid process is well-documented (CITE)"

**Proposed citation:** Dynarski and Scott-Clayton (2006) and Bettinger et al. (2012)

- Dynarski, S. and Scott-Clayton, J. (2006). The Cost of Complexity in Federal Student Aid: Lessons from Optimal Tax Theory and Behavioral Economics. *National Tax Journal*, 59(2), 319-356. — Foundational paper showing FAFSA is as complex as IRS tax forms; complexity deters low-income students.
- Bettinger, E.P., Long, B.T., Oreopoulos, P., and Sanbonmatsu, L. (2012). The Role of Application Assistance and Information in College Decisions: Results from the H&R Block FAFSA Experiment. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 127(3), 1205-1242. — RCT showing FAFSA completion assistance increased college enrollment. Top-5 journal, causal evidence.

**Proposed text:** "The complexity of the financial aid process is well-documented (Dynarski and Scott-Clayton, 2006; Bettinger et al., 2012), including the initial FAFSA application process."

### Change 2: CITE or soften political climate claim (Challenges section)

**Location:** "The current political climate may have heightened concerns about disclosing sensitive family information, particularly for undocumented students or students from mixed-status households (CITE)."

**Finding:** No peer-reviewed research directly on immigration enforcement and FAFSA completion. However, strong "chilling effects" literature from adjacent domains supports the claim by analogy:

- Watson, T. (2014). Inside the Refrigerator: Immigration Enforcement and Chilling Effects in Medicaid Participation. *AEJ: Economic Policy*, 6(3), 313-338. — Enforcement reduced Medicaid participation among children of noncitizens.
- Alsan, M. and Yang, C. (2022). Fear and the Safety Net: Evidence from Secure Communities. *Review of Economics and Statistics*, 104(6), 1123-1141. — Secure Communities led to ~17% decline in health care visits among lawfully present Hispanics.

Policy/news sources directly on FAFSA fears:

- CalMatters (Dec 2024): "Students with undocumented parents fear FAFSA as Trump returns" — documents the exact concern among CA mixed-status families.
- NILC (2024): "Key Points to Know About FAFSA Data Security" — legal analysis of FAFSA data protections and risks.

**Proposed text (softened + cited):** "Consistent with a well-documented pattern in which immigration enforcement reduces immigrant families' engagement with government programs (Watson, 2014; Alsan and Yang, 2022), respondents in 2025 were more likely to be concerned about sharing their family's information with the federal or state government than they were in 2024, with the percentage of respondents citing this concern rising from 12% to 17%."

This reframes the claim from speculation ("the current political climate may have") to established pattern ("consistent with well-documented pattern"), supported by peer-reviewed research.

**Proposed footnote** (attach to end of sentence):
> Recent reporting has documented these concerns among California families specifically. See "Students with undocumented parents fear FAFSA as Trump returns," CalMatters, December 2024; see also National Immigration Law Center, "Key Points to Know About FAFSA Data Security," 2024, for an overview of current legal protections and risks regarding FAFSA data and immigration enforcement.
